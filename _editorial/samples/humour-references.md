# Humour references: techniques, not text to copy

Vanshaj shared these LinkedIn posts as models for the humour he wants. They belong to their authors. They are summarised here for technique only. **Never reuse their lines or premises in a post.**

## 1. "The AI agent that found the least productive employee" (viral LinkedIn post, author unattributed)

**What happens:** A CEO hires an AI specialist to find low performers. The specialist builds an agent that trawls months of company docs, emails and tasks. The agent's answer is the CEO himself. Final line: the AI specialist is the one who gets fired.

**Why it works:**
- **Deadpan corporate setup.** It reads like a real LinkedIn success story until the last two lines.
- **Fake precision** ("9 months of data", "20 hours of work") makes the absurd sound credible.
- **Double twist.** First the tool turns on its owner. Then the consequence lands on the wrong person. Each twist is a one-line paragraph.
- **The joke isn't explained.** The reader does the work, which is what makes them share it.
- **The hidden point,** which is why Vanshaj likes it: AI can produce the "right" answer, but humans decide what happens next, and power, politics and accountability stay human. The post makes the case for humans in the AI loop without saying so.

## 2. Ansh Jain on GenZ and phone calls (LinkedIn post)

**What happens:** A manager is exasperated that GenZ colleagues text instead of calling, even for things that need a call (delayed projects, rescheduled meetings, nuance). He imagines being told by text that the office is on fire, admits he feels like "an uncle" for insisting on calls, contrasts this with his parents' generation over-calling, and concludes that millennials are the only generation that uses both properly.

**Why it works:**
- **Relatable observation.** Every manager has lived it.
- **The exaggerated hypothetical** (the fire-by-text) is where the laugh happens.
- **Mimicking the voice.** The imagined text is written in drawn-out GenZ spelling.
- **Self-deprecation** ("such an uncle") keeps it warm, not preachy.
- **One CAPS mock-rant line** for comic emphasis, used once.
- **The rule of three across generations,** with the punchline on the writer's own generation, so nobody is singled out.
- **A real workplace point underneath:** some conversations need a call. The humour carries a genuine management lesson.

## How the agent should use this
Study the structure, then write original jokes about the HR or business topic at hand. A good test: if the joke would make sense only to someone who has read these two posts, it's borrowed, not original.
