# Sample: leadership post (Vanshaj's own writing, reproduced as he wrote it)

Ben Stokes! 

Among the most tenacious players to have graced this game, Stokes's leadership often lurks in the shadows, perhaps overshadowed by his own greatness as a player. Yet, let's unravel a chapter where he gambled on debutant Tom Hartley. 

Just picture this scene: You have a debutant in your team, making his debut against cricket giants, India, on their formidable home turf. And, the opener has gone berserk against his bowling, absolutely smashing him all over the park, adding to your misery.

Did this make Ben Stokes, the leader, flinch? No! He let Hartley weather the storm, ending with a modest 131-2 in 25 overs.

Many cricket pundits criticized his move of giving Hartley 25 overs, saying that maybe Stokes went a little too far in backing his debutant player and that he should not have given him more than 10 overs.

Fast forward to the second innings, and behold! Hartley, the nervous debutant, had transformed into a spin wizard, spinning the ball with absolute venom, like a magician conjuring spells and ended up outclassing Indian batsmen with a golden figure of 7-62, driving his team towards a famous win.

So, what sparked this turnaround?

Stokes's unwavering faith in the potential of Hartley. Despite the initial setback, Stokes believed that if he continues backing Hartley, this will boost his confidence, ultimately enabling him to unleash his talent and show the world what he can help his team truly achieve. He knew that his backing might not pay off in the first innings, but ultimately, it would.

This exemplifies the essence of strong leadership. When a leader recognizes the potential within their teammates, they back that individual despite their failures to uplift their confidence because they know that this confidence will ultimately empower the individual to unleash their potential and drive the team towards success.
