# Editorial rules

These rules are non-negotiable. The drafting agent must follow every one.

## Reader
HR heads, HRBPs and business leaders in Indian tech, GCCs, IT services and fast-growing companies. Smart, busy, sceptical of generic HR content.

## Positioning
Every post should show that the author understands the business first and HR second. Order of thought: business model → business problem → people problem → HR intervention → business outcome → how to measure it.

## Content pillars (draft — Vanshaj to confirm)
1. **Commercial HR** — business models, unit economics and what they demand of people strategy.
2. **People analytics for HRBPs** — metrics leaders actually act on, and how to present them.
3. **The HRBP craft** — stakeholder management, performance, org design, employee relations, change.
4. **AI and the future of HR work** — practical, evidence-based, no hype.

## Format
- 900–1,300 words.
- A title that promises a specific insight, plus a one-line subtitle.
- Open with a concrete situation or tension, not a definition.
- At least one worked example with realistic (clearly hypothetical) numbers.
- End with a short, usable takeaway: a framework, checklist or set of questions.
- Markdown only. Use headers sparingly; prefer prose over long bullet lists.

## Evidence
- Every statistic, survey result or market claim must link to its source, preferably primary (company reports, government data, consulting firms, LinkedIn/Naukri data, reputable business press).
- Sources must be recent for anything describing the current market; label older sources as historical.
- Separate fact from opinion. Opinion is welcome and should be marked as the author's view.
- Never quote more than one short sentence from any source. Paraphrase.
- If a claim can't be sourced, cut it.

## About the author
- Never invent experience, achievements, numbers, employers or projects for Vanshaj.
- Where a personal example would strengthen the post, insert a placeholder exactly like this and leave it for Vanshaj to fill:
  `> [YOUR EXAMPLE: one sentence on what would fit here]`
- Never use confidential employer material (internal documents, role notes, org charts, unreleased numbers).

## Tone
Smart and funny. Humour follows the rules in `style-guide.md` (Humour section). The jokes open the door; the evidence and analysis have to be solid enough to stand without them.

## Disclosure
Every post ends with: *Researched and drafted with AI assistance; reviewed and edited by the author.*

## Banned
- Generic openers ("In today's fast-paced world…", "HR is evolving…").
- Buzzword stacking ("synergy", "leverage" as a verb, "game-changer", "unlock potential").
- Listicles with no argument.
- Naming or criticising current or former employers.
